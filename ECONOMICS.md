# ECONOMICS

## Overview

StackSpend is fundamentally a lead-generation and trust-building product for Credex.

The goal is not direct monetization from audits themselves. The product becomes valuable when:

1. a company discovers meaningful AI tooling waste
2. Credex helps optimize or finance those purchases
3. the customer becomes a recurring infrastructure or credits buyer

The economics below assume Credex uses StackSpend primarily as a top-of-funnel acquisition engine.

---

# Estimated Value of a Converted Lead

I estimate a converted Credex customer is worth approximately:

> $3,000–$12,000 ARR

depending on company size and AI infrastructure usage.

Reasoning:

* small startups may spend only a few hundred dollars monthly on AI tooling and API usage
* engineering-heavy startups can easily spend several thousand dollars monthly on OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, and related infrastructure
* if Credex captures even a small percentage of those purchases through credits or procurement workflows, the account becomes meaningful quickly

For modeling purposes, I assume:

* average converted customer value:

  * $6,000 ARR
* gross margin estimate:

  * 40%

This produces approximately:

> $2,400 gross profit per converted customer annually

---

# CAC by Acquisition Channel

## 1. Organic X / Twitter Threads

Expected CAC:

> $20–$80 per qualified lead

Reasoning:

* founder/operator audiences already discuss AI tooling costs publicly
* screenshot-worthy reports naturally support sharing
* distribution cost is primarily time, not paid spend

This is likely the cheapest early-stage channel.

---

## 2. Hacker News / Indie Hackers

Expected CAC:

> $50–$150 per qualified lead

Reasoning:

* high-quality technical traffic
* lower volume but strong intent
* users are highly relevant to AI tooling optimization

---

## 3. Founder Communities / Slack Groups

Examples:

* AI engineer communities
* startup operator Slacks
* indie hacker groups

Expected CAC:

> $75–$200 per qualified lead

These channels require more manual outreach but likely produce higher-intent users.

---

# Funnel Economics

Assume:

## Monthly Funnel

* 10,000 landing page visitors
* 35% audit completion rate
* 3,500 completed audits

Of completed audits:

* 20% identify meaningful savings
* 700 high-savings audits

Of high-savings audits:

* 12% book consultations
* 84 consultation calls

Of consultation calls:

* 20% convert into ongoing credit/procurement relationships
* ~17 customers

---

# Revenue Projection

Using:

* $6,000 ARR per converted customer

Monthly generated ARR:

> 17 × $6,000 = $102,000 ARR

Annualized:

> approximately $1.2M ARR

---

# What Must Be True To Reach $1M ARR in 18 Months

For this tool to realistically drive $1M ARR:

## 1. Public Sharing Must Work

The public report experience must generate:

* screenshots
* reposts
* founder/operator discussion

Without low-cost organic distribution, CAC increases significantly.

---

## 2. The Audit Must Identify Real Savings

The product only works if:

* recommendations feel financially credible
* savings are meaningful enough to create urgency
* users trust the deterministic logic

If most audits identify only trivial savings, conversion intent will remain low.

---

## 3. Credex Must Capture Procurement Workflows

The long-term value comes from:

* API credit purchases
* centralized procurement
* AI spend management workflows

The audit itself is not the business model — it is the acquisition mechanism.

---

# Profitability Threshold

The funnel becomes highly profitable if:

* audit completion rate stays above 30%
* consultation booking rate exceeds 10%
* customer conversion after consultation remains above 15%

At those levels, even relatively modest organic traffic can sustain strong unit economics because the downstream account value is significantly larger than acquisition cost.
